2026 · Qinghai, Gansu & Tibet The G109 to Tibet

Venturing solo deep into China's most remote regions. Exploring Hoh Xil on the Qinghai Tibet Plateau via the entirely off road G109, attempting to reach the Tibet border with no guide or permit, visiting the source of the Yangtze and exploring everything along the way. Three weeks and 6,000 km alone through Qinghai, Gansu and Tibet.

“The Qinghai-Tibet Highway, the shortest route from anywhere into Tibet, was no easy trip.” “Of the thousands of trucks that plied the route each day, at least two would slide off the road, roll over mountainous embankments, or slam into one another as drivers battled ice, fog, and fatigue.” “You know you are suffering the effects of altitude when the frontal lobes of your brain start a coordinated drum solo with the base of your skull.”Abrahm Lustgarten, China’s Great Train

2024 · South Xinjiang · 4,645 km · 12 days Tasha Gudao

Crossing and documenting Tasha Gudao, an ancient road hidden deep in the valleys of the Kunlun Mountains, found by tracing satellite imagery, and the first known crossing by a foreigner. Then the Karakoram Highway, the Pamirs and the Taklamakan Desert.

“Tasha Gudao’s reputation for danger has even overshadowed the magnificence of its scenery.” “A perfectly good numbered county road, yet no navigation app will route you along it, and its complicated conditions keep the vast majority of people at a respectful distance.”塔莎古道险峻的名声，甚至盖过了他风景的壮美。好端端的一条有编号的县道，却所有导航都不会让你走，复杂的路况，也让绝大多数人对他敬而远之。Sohu, 2021, translated from Chinese

2024 to 2026 · Guizhou, Yunnan & Sichuan · 3 trips The world's highest bridges

Documenting the highest bridges on Earth in Guizhou, the ‘kingdom of bridges’, where mountains cover 92.5% of the provincial land area. The 15 highest bridges in the world are all in China, and reaching them all by road takes a person to incredible, fascinating, unknown places. Currently in pursuit of visiting them all, with 16/26 of the highest bridges in China visited.

“…you cannot drive to these high bridges yourself.” “First, do not even think of driving or renting a car in China’s western provinces.”HighestBridges.com, advice on visiting the bridges

Doing it anyway: self-driven, with no hired driver, guide or interpreter.

Exploring China

The unfathomable scale of China

The world's biggest everything is there: the highest bridges, the largest highway and high speed rail networks, the busiest ports, the biggest telescope and renewable energy industry, most of it built in the last 20 years.

With a car, an entire new travel map opens up, out to the farthest corners and the places and projects in them, through landscapes that rival Switzerland, Norway, the USA and the Sahara, all in one country.

So far I have driven 30,000km through deepest China, and visited 24 provinces and over 100 cities and towns in pursuit of exploring the country as far and wide as possible. And with years of trips planned ahead, there is no end in sight.
